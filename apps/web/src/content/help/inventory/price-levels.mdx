A **price level** is a named list of selling prices, such as **Retail**, **Wholesale** or **Dealer**. Give each customer a level, and their invoices automatically pick up the right prices. Items without a price on a level simply sell at their own sale price.

Open it from the sidebar: **Inventory → Price Levels**.

## Creating a price level

1. Click **+ New level** (or **Create a price level** if you have none yet).
2. Enter a **Name**, for example "Wholesale", and an optional **Description**.
3. Tick **Default level** if customers without a level of their own, and walk-in sales, should be billed at it. Only one level can be the default; choosing a new one clears the old one.
4. Save.

Each level appears as a card showing its name, a **Default** badge if it's the default, how many prices it has and how many customers use it. Use **Edit** or **Delete** on the card.

Deleting a level removes its prices. Its customers are then billed at the default level.

## Setting prices

The page shows a price grid:

| Column | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Item** | Each stock item and its unit. Variants appear as indented rows. |
| **Sale price** | The item's own sale price |
| **MRP** | The item's MRP, if set |
| One column per level | The price on that level. Type to change it. |

- A blank cell means the item sells at its own sale price on that level. The sale price is shown faintly in the cell as a reminder.
- Changed cells are highlighted. Click **Save N changes** to save them all, or **Discard**.
- Prices can have up to 2 decimals.
- A price above the item's MRP gets an amber border as a warning.

Use the search box to find an item.

### Quantity slabs and dated prices

Click **Slabs…** under a cell to open the slab editor for that item on that level. Here you can:

- **Set quantity slabs.** Add rows with **From qty**, and a **Rate (₹)** and/or **Discount %**. The highest slab a line's quantity reaches applies. For example: from 1 → ₹100, from 10 → ₹95, from 50 → ₹90. A row with only a discount keeps the item's own price and fills in the line discount instead.
- **Price an alternate unit.** If the item has [alternate units](/help/items/units-and-conversions), choose the **Unit**, for example a separate rate per box.
- **Schedule a price change.** Set **Effective from** to a date. Invoices dated on or after it use the new prices; earlier invoices keep the old ones.

Existing unit and date versions are listed under **Saved**. Click **Save slabs**. A cell with extra slabs, units or dates shows a link such as "+2 slab/unit/dated".

### Bulk update

To change a whole level at once, click **Bulk update**:

| Field | Options |
|---|---|
| **Price level** | The level to update |
| **Start from** | **Items' sale prices** (sets each item's price on the level) or **The level's current prices** |
| **Change (%)** | For example −10 for 10% below, 5 for 5% above |
| **Rounding** | **Keep paise** or **Round to the nearest rupee** |
| **Only category** | Limit to one category (type its exact name), or leave blank for all items |

Click **Update prices**. Prices never go below zero.

Setting up wholesale for the first time? Create a "Wholesale" level, run **Bulk update** starting from **Items' sale prices** with −8%, and round to the nearest rupee. Then fix the few items that need a special price in the grid.

## Giving a customer a price level

Open the customer on the **Parties** page and choose a **Price level**. When adding a customer, it's in the **Credit Terms** section; on an existing customer you can change it directly on the detail view. Choose **Default** to use the business default level.

The option only appears for customers, and only once you have at least one price level.

## How prices are picked on an invoice

On sale documents (invoices, quotations, proforma invoices, sales orders, delivery challans and debit notes), Fintranzact fills in each line's price like this:

1. **Which level:** the customer's level, otherwise the default level. With no level, the item's own sale price is used.
2. **Which entry:** prices for the line's unit come first. For an alternate unit with no price of its own, the base-unit price is multiplied by the conversion.
3. **Which date:** the latest price effective on or before the document date.
4. **Which slab:** the highest slab the line's quantity reaches.
5. If nothing matches, the item's own sale price is used.

Below the lines, a caption shows which level is being used, for example "Prices from the Wholesale price level". Prices update as you change the customer, date, item, unit or quantity. A price you type yourself is never overwritten, and neither are lines on a document you open for editing.

Price levels apply to sales only. Purchases always use the item's purchase price. Credit notes and sales returns aren't re-priced.

## MRP

Record an item's printed maximum retail price under **MRP** on the item form (**MRP (₹)**). Leave it blank if the item has no MRP.

- If the sale price is above the MRP, the item form shows a warning. The same warning appears under an invoice line priced above the MRP. It's a warning only; saving isn't blocked.
- On sale invoice PDFs, the MRP is printed under the item name ("MRP ₹…"), converted to the line's unit.
- The item detail shows the MRP.
- MRP is **not** printed on barcode labels.

The [Price List](/help/inventory/reports) report shows every item's sale price, MRP and price on each level, for any date.

## Frequently asked questions

**A customer has no price level. Which price do they get?**
The default level's price, if you've marked one as default; otherwise the item's own sale price.

**Can I give one customer a special price on one item?**
Price levels work per group of customers. For a one-off price, type it on the invoice line; a typed price is never replaced.

**Will changing a price level change old invoices?**
No. Prices are filled in when you create the document. Use **Effective from** to schedule changes for a future date.

**Can I sell above MRP?**
Fintranzact warns you but doesn't stop you. Selling above the printed MRP isn't allowed by law, so correct the price.
