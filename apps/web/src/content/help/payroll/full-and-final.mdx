When an employee leaves, record the exit on the **Employees** tab (last working day and reason), then settle in **Payroll → Full and final**.

## The last month's salary is not in the settlement

The salary for the month of the last working day is paid by the **normal payroll run of that month**. That run already pays for the days worked and takes PF, ESI, professional tax and TDS, and the payslip and statutory files stay complete. The settlement links to that run, shows what it pays, and **cannot be submitted until that run is approved**. This is how the last month is never paid twice and never missed. If there is no run for the month yet, create and approve it first.

## What the settlement contains

**Amounts due**

- **Leave encashment**: for each leave type that is encashable, the balance in the leave year of the last working day (limited by the type's carry-forward maximum), times a daily rate. You choose the rate: Basic + DA / 26 (the default), Basic + DA / 30, or gross / 30. You can lower the days. Check the basis with your CA.
- **Gratuity**: as in [Gratuity](/help/payroll/gratuity), using the last working day and the exit reason.
- **Bonus due** (optional): a suggestion at the lowest percentage, from the approved payroll of the financial year so far. It is left out if a bonus run already pays it.
- **Arrears and other amounts**: lines you add.

**Recoveries**

- **Notice-period shortfall**: you enter the days short; the recovery is the gross monthly salary / 30 for each day.
- **TDS on the settlement**: **not calculated**. Enter the amount yourself after checking with your CA.
- **Other recoveries**: lines you add.
- **Loans and advances**: the outstanding **principal** of every active loan is recovered **last**, from what is left, and never takes the net payable below zero. Any part that cannot be recovered stays outstanding on the loan, with a warning. While a settlement exists for an employee, payroll runs do not deduct their loan instalments.

**Net payable** = amounts due - recoveries - loans.

## Steps

1. **+ New settlement** and choose the employee. It is calculated at once.
2. Change the inputs and press **Save and recalculate** (draft only). **Recalculate** also refreshes leave and loan balances.
3. **Submit for approval**. An **owner or admin**, who is not the person who last calculated it (unless your business has one user), approves. Approving records the leave encashed and the loans recovered, and freezes the figures.
4. **Post to books** and **Mark as paid** (owner, admin or accountant).
5. **Statement (PDF)** gives the employee a settlement statement at any time after the first calculation.

An employee with a live settlement that is more than a draft cannot be brought back with Reactivate.

## Reversing a posted settlement

If a settlement was posted by mistake (the wrong employee, the wrong date or wrong figures), an **owner, admin or accountant** (never HR) can press **Reverse settlement** on a posted settlement. You must give a **reason**, which is kept on the record and in the audit log. Reversing:

- posts a mirror entry that **exactly negates** the original (the original entry is marked voided and points to it, and the date is the same, so the period must not be locked);
- **puts the loan balances back**: the loans it recovered are active again with the balance restored (the loan history keeps both lines; nothing is deleted) and a new repayment schedule starts next month at the same EMI;
- **gives the encashed leave back** to the balance;
- gives the **gratuity back to the Gratuity Provision** (the entry drew it) and **frees the bonus**, so a bonus run takes the employee in again;
- turns the settlement into **Reversed**, which is final. Its statement PDF stays available.

It does **not** bring the employee back: they stay shown as having left. Start a **new settlement** from the list (a reversed one does not count as the employee's settlement) and the loans are recovered in it as before. Reactivating the employee first is a separate decision for HR and is allowed once the settlement is reversed.

A settlement that has been **paid** cannot be reversed straight away. Press **Reverse payment** first (reason required): the money goes back into the account it was paid from, a mirror entry negates the payment entry, and the settlement returns to **Posted to books**. You can then mark it paid again, or reverse the settlement. An approved settlement that is not posted yet cannot be reversed; post it and then reverse it, or correct it with a journal entry. Entries a settlement posted cannot be voided by hand from Journal entries: use these buttons so the loans and leave are put back too.

## Books

Dr leave encashment and other dues (Salary - Allowances), arrears (Salary & Wages), bonus (Salary - Bonus & Incentives), gratuity (the Gratuity Provision, then Salary - Gratuity) / Cr Full and Final Settlements Payable (the net), Loans and Advances to Employees, Payroll Deductions Payable and TDS on Salary Payable. Paying clears Full and Final Settlements Payable against the bank.
